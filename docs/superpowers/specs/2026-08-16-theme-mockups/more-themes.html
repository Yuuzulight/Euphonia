Paper
Warm oat-cream instead of white. Lower glare than any light theme here, and the pastel zone colors sit on it beautifully — cream is the classic ground for pastels.

Deep indigo — the most conventional "calm dark". Reads as serious rather than cozy.
⚠️ Navy chrome sits closest to the blue that means "masculine end". Distinguishable here, but it's the weakest separation of the six.

Amber night
Very warm, almost no blue light — the "practising at 1am without waking yourself up" theme. Strongest candidate if evening use is when you actually record.
⚠️ The warm cast slightly mutes the blue data color. Least accurate for judging fine color differences in charts.

Focus
All chrome goes neutral gray so the only color on screen is your data. The zone bars pop harder here than in any other theme. Could ship in both a light and dark flavor.

High contrast
A genuine accessibility theme for low vision — every pairing clears WCAG AAA. Not pretty, and not what you asked for, but it's the one that makes the app usable for people the pastels currently exclude.
⚠️ The only theme that must override the data colors — pastels can't hit AAA. Same hues, saturated. A deliberate exception to the rule in Section 1.

My honest advice on how many to ship: each extra theme is cheap to define but not free to verify — every one needs a pass over modals, charts, the waveform and the desktop title bar. Four is comfortable. Adding paper and cocoa would give you a genuinely well-rounded set of six (two light, two dark, plus a warm one at each end) without much risk. I'd treat high contrast as a separate piece of work rather than folding it in here — it's an accessibility feature with its own rules, and burying it in a theme sweep is how it ends up half-done.
